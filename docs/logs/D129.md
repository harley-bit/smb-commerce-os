## What happened

Built "Checkout: deposit consent" as the deposit-terms counterpart to D128's terms-of-service consent: `docs/design/07`'s repudiation mitigation for "Customer denies agreeing to deposit terms" is the same shape as its ToS mitigation ("explicit consent captured at checkout with version of terms and timestamp"), but no code anywhere disclosed or recorded consent to a rental deposit hold specifically. New pure-domain `buildDepositDisclosure` (`packages/domain/src/payments/depositDisclosure.ts`) computes the hold amount, duration (whole days, rounded up, from the order line's own `startsAt`/`endsAt`), and a fixed set of plain-language conditions (not a charge until captured; released automatically on a timely, on-condition return; may be partially/fully captured for damage/loss/late fees; expires automatically) entirely from a rental order line's own server-recorded `depositMinor`/dates and the order's own `currency` — never from client input. New table `deposit_consents` (both dialects, migrations `0021_flawless_miracleman.sql`/`0023_fixed_nuke.sql`) records one append-only row per acknowledgement, with the disclosed amount/currency/duration captured on the row itself so the evidence survives even if the order line's own deposit or dates change later. New `DepositConsentRepository` (`record`/`findLatestForOrderLine`, tenant-scoped) and `CheckoutDepositConsentService.acknowledgeDeposit` — same shape as D128's `CheckoutDetailsService`: validates order ownership (`NotOrderOwnerError`) and `CART`/`PENDING_PAYMENT` status (`OrderNotCheckoutableError`), validates the order line belongs to the order (`OrderLineNotFoundError`), computes the disclosure server-side, records the consent row, and mirrors the same disclosure into the merchant's hash-chained `audit_log` (`checkout.deposit_consent_recorded`). Same open gap as D121–D128: no HTTP route or storefront UI exists yet, since no checkout endpoint has been wired at all through this phase — the service is ready for D130/D131 to call once payment UI/checkout wiring lands.

## Acceptance criteria check

- "Customer sees hold amount, duration, and conditions" — **Met.** `CheckoutDepositConsentService.acknowledgeDeposit` returns a `DepositConsentResult` carrying `holdAmountMinor`, `currency`, `holdDurationDays`, and the full `conditions` array, all computed server-side before consent is recorded — proven by `CheckoutDepositConsentService.test.ts`'s first case and by `depositDisclosure.test.ts` at the pure-function level.

## Security check answer

"Clear disclosure reduces dispute and chargeback risk": a customer who was shown the exact hold amount, duration, and capture/release conditions before consenting has a materially weaker basis to later claim the hold or a subsequent capture was an unauthorized or surprise charge. If they dispute it anyway, `deposit_consents` plus its `audit_log` mirror (same hash-chained, tamper-evident mechanism `CheckoutDetailsService`/D128 uses for terms consent) is the merchant's evidence of exactly what was disclosed and when — a plain `deposit_consents` row alone could be quietly edited after the fact by a privileged actor with DB access; the audit-log mirror is what makes a later "the customer was never shown this" claim checkable against a chain that would visibly break if altered.
